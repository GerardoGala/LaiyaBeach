🌬️ Wind Condition

The forecast may report a steady 9 mph north wind, but the shoreline tells a different story. Local terrain reshapes the breeze into zones of calm and acceleration. Understanding these effects is essential for sailors who want to anticipate how the boat will behave between the beach and the buoy.

The Shoreline Shadow

At the beach, the north wind is muted by houses and trees standing just inland. These obstacles break the flow into turbulence and pockets of dead air. Sails hang slack, progress stalls, and pumping may be the only way to escape the calm. This is the wind shadow zone, where the land itself steals the breeze.

The Offshore Funnel

Roughly 500 meters out, near the buoy, the hills rise behind the shoreline. As the north wind clears these slopes, it accelerates — funneled and compressed by the terrain. The breeze that was gentle at the forecast station becomes a driving force offshore. The boat heels, hiking becomes essential, and the sailor feels the sudden lift in the sail. This is the acceleration zone, where terrain amplifies the wind.

Simulation Rule

  • North winds (330°–30°):
    • Beach zone: wind reduced (×0.5).
    • Buoy zone (~500 m SE): wind increased (×1.3).
  • Other directions: API wind unchanged.

Notes for Sailors

Expect calm near shore, stronger breeze offshore. The transition is not gradual — it is a sudden shift, like stepping from shadow into sunlight. The map scale bar confirms the buoy lies exactly 500 meters out, making this effect measurable and repeatable in the simulation.
