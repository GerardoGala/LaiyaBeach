⚓ Physics of Sailing

The following is a practical guideline on how to gain the maximum Velocity Made Good (VMG) from an ILCA dinghy. VMG is the measure of how efficiently you are moving toward your destination, not just how fast the boat is going. Every adjustment you make—whether it’s trimming the sheet, setting the daggerboard, shifting your weight, or fine‑tuning the sail controls—affects VMG. Think of these notes as a playbook: they explain how each control should be used across different points of sail—from close‑hauled, beam reach, broad reach, to running—balancing speed, height, and stability. Mastering these fundamentals will let you squeeze every knot of performance out of the boat, whether you’re racing or simply trying to sail with precision.

Sheet (Boom Angle)

  • Close‑hauled: Sheet in tight to maximize lift and point high. In a puff: ease slightly to prevent stalling and excessive heel.
  • Beam reach: Ease slightly for balanced power and speed. In a puff: ease quickly to dump excess power, then trim back in.
  • Broad reach: Ease more to keep flow attached and prevent stalling. In a puff: ease further to avoid rounding up; maintain smooth flow.
  • Running: Sheet way out; sail acts like a parachute catching wind. In a puff: ease aggressively or steer slightly to keep control and avoid broach.

Daggerboard

  • 2 is all the way up. 0 is middle. -2 is all the way down.
  • Close‑hauled: Fully down to resist leeway.
  • Beam reach: Mostly down for balance.
  • Broad reach: Raise slightly to reduce drag.
  • Running: Raise more; lateral resistance less critical.

Sailor Position

  • Close‑hauled: Hike hard to counter heel.
  • Beam reach: Moderate hiking; adjust fore/aft for waves.
  • Broad reach: Shift aft to keep bow up and stable.
  • Running: Stay centered or aft; stability more important than hiking.

Vang (Kicker)

  • Close‑hauled: Tight to flatten sail and control twist.
  • Beam reach: Moderate tension; balance power and control.
  • Broad reach: Ease to allow twist and spill excess wind.
  • Running: Very loose; sail needs maximum twist to avoid collapse.

Downhaul (Cunningham)

  • Close‑hauled: Tight to move draft forward, flatten sail, reduce drag.
  • Beam reach: Moderate depth; balance speed and control.
  • Broad reach: Looser for fuller sail shape and more power.
  • Running: Very loose; deep draft maximizes drive.

Outhaul

  • Close‑hauled: Tight for flat sail, efficient upwind.
  • Beam reach: Moderate; balance speed and power.
  • Broad reach: Looser for fuller sail, more drive.
  • Running: Very loose; maximum depth for downwind push.

ILCA Heading (Pinching vs. Footing)

  • Close‑hauled: Pinching gains height but risks stalling; footing gains speed but loses height.
  • Beam reach: Heading is stable; small adjustments optimize speed.
  • Broad reach: Footing slightly off the wind increases speed; pinching less relevant.
  • Running: Heading directly downwind; pinching/footing not applicable.
