ILCA Pro-Tip Strategy Manual

To master the leaderboard, you must know how Olympic-level sailors configure their boat trim. Optimize your 6 structural controls across all wind scales and headings to unlock maximum speed.

1. Upwind Tactics (Close Hauled)

Light to Medium Wind (0-14 kn)

  • Boom Angle: 0° to 8° (blocks split in light air; tight block-to-block in medium).
  • Sailor Position: Mid-Forward to Mid-Cockpit; lean leeward early, then hike hard.
  • Daggerboard: Fully Down (2) for strict tracking.
  • Vang: Slack to Firm; take out play early, then firm up to block boom rise.
  • Cunningham: Loose to Snug; let loose wrinkles hold power, then erase folds.
  • Outhaul: Base; hand-width gap to moderate shape for power.

Heavy Wind (15+ kn)

  • Boom Angle: 5° to 15° (play actively in gusts).
  • Sailor Position: Max Hike Out; hold boat flat.
  • Daggerboard: Up 2-3 inches; stops hull trip forces.
  • Vang: Max Tight; flattens middle of the sail.
  • Cunningham: Max Down; pulls sail draft forward.
  • Outhaul: Flat; rigidly pulled tight to aluminum.

2. Reaching Tactics

Light to Medium Wind (0-14 kn)

  • Boom Angle: 35° to 65° (trim to telltales and track target course path).
  • Sailor Position: Forward to Mid-Cockpit; clear out transom drag and hike on waves.
  • Daggerboard: Halfway Up (0) to shed friction and balance helm.
  • Vang: Slack to Mid-Tension; let boom lift cleanly, or set top batten parallel.
  • Cunningham: Off to Snugged slightly; unlock and then lock shape profile.
  • Outhaul: Full to Base; deep driving pocket to moderate depth for force.

Heavy Wind (15+ kn)

  • Boom Angle: 40° to 70° (dump fast if rolling over).
  • Sailor Position: Far Aft; keeps bow out of water.
  • Daggerboard: Up Significantly (-1) to drop drag.
  • Vang: Heavy tension; stabilizes sail dump.
  • Cunningham: Hard down; strips power from sail head.
  • Outhaul: Pulled flat; reduces high aerodynamic drag.

3. Downwind Tactics (Running)

Light Wind (0-7 kn)

  • Boom Angle: 75° to 85° (never push past 90°).
  • Sailor Position: Forward; heel hull to windward.
  • Daggerboard: Up High (-1) to minimize drag.
  • Vang: Eased; holds boom from dipping low.
  • Cunningham: Eased completely off.
  • Outhaul: Full; eased deep for broad cross-section.

Medium to Heavy Wind (8+ kn)

  • Boom Angle: 90° to 110° (lock into deep "by-the-lee" sailing).
  • Sailor Position: Mid-Cockpit to Transom Wall; active surfing stance, ready to shift weight.
  • Daggerboard: Halfway Down to 3/4 Up (-1 to -1.5) to clear drag and stabilize.
  • Vang: Tight to Open; set leech to twist or crank hard to prevent high-speed death-rolls.
  • Cunningham: Eased to 1/4 Down; clears entry profile in heavy gusts.
  • Outhaul: Full to Flat; maximize curve or flatten bottom panels to stabilize.

4. Tactical Mastery: Reading Wind Shifts

Speed is useless if you are sailing the wrong way! When tracking upwind (Close Hauled), the wind will constantly shift. Your strategy must change instantly based on what the wind does.

💨 The Lift (Favorable Shift)

What it is: The wind shifts further behind your boat.

The Result: You can turn closer toward your target upwind mark without your sails flapping.

Strategy: Stay on this tack! Ride the lift as long as it lasts to steal a shorter path to the buoy.

💨 The Header (Unfavorable Shift)

What it is: The wind shifts straight into your bow.

The Result: You are forced to turn your boat away from your target upwind mark to keep your sails full.

Strategy: Tack immediately! A header on your current side means a lift is waiting for you on the other side.

🏆 The Golden Upwind Rule

"Tack on a header, stay on a lift!"

Sailing a straight line away from a shift leaves you stranded. Watch your compass angle, feel the header, and tack quickly to stay on the inside track.

5. Boat Handling: Maneuver Penalties

Sailing fast requires smooth boat handling. Avoid unnecessary turns and sloppy maneuvers, as jerky movements drag through the water and kill your forward momentum.
