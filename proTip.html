ILCA Pro-Tip Strategy Manual

To master the leaderboard, you must know how Olympic-level sailors configure their boat trim. Optimize your 6 structural controls across all wind scales and headings to unlock maximum speed.

1. Upwind Tactics (Close Hauled)

Light to Medium Wind (0-14 kn)

  • Boom Angle: 0° to 8° (blocks split in light air; tight block-to-block in medium).
  • Sailor Position: Forward (in light air) or Hike Hard (in medium wind).
  • Daggerboard: Down for strict upwind tracking.
  • Vang: Ease (light air) or Center (medium wind).
  • Downhaul: Base; removes loose wrinkles to balance luff tension.
  • Outhaul: Base; holds moderate shape for optimal driving power.

Heavy Wind (15+ kn)

  • Boom Angle: 5° to 15° (play actively in gusts to dump excess load).
  • Sailor Position: Hike Hard; lean out completely flat to counter the breeze.
  • Daggerboard: Down; maximizes upwind lift and tracking efficiency.
  • Vang: Max; locks the boom down hard to flatten the sail center.
  • Downhaul: Max Luff; pulled down tight to drag the sail draft forward.
  • Outhaul: Flat; eliminates pocket depth to minimize aerodynamic drag.

2. Reaching Tactics

Light to Medium Wind (0-14 kn)

  • Boom Angle: 35° to 65° (trim to telltales and track target course path).
  • Sailor Position: Forward (light air) or Hike Hard (medium wind) to track waves.
  • Daggerboard: Center; reduces skin friction drag while maintaining steerage.
  • Vang: Ease (light air) or Center (medium wind); lets the boom rise cleanly.
  • Downhaul: Off (light air) or Base (medium wind); unlocks full shape volume.
  • Outhaul: Full (light air) or Base (medium wind); deep driving air pocket.

Heavy Wind (15+ kn)

  • Boom Angle: 40° to 70° (dump fast if rolling over).
  • Sailor Position: Aft; moves body weight backward to clear bow transom drag.
  • Daggerboard: Center; maintains tracking control against heavy sail loads.
  • Vang: Max; stabilizes the leech profile during heavy wind dumps.
  • Downhaul: Base; stabilizes entry shape against heavy gusts.
  • Outhaul: Flat; reduces drag forces on fast reaching angles.

3. Downwind Tactics (Running)

Light Wind (0-7 kn)

  • Boom Angle: 75° to 85° (never push past 90°).
  • Sailor Position: Forward; shifts boat weight into light air pockets.
  • Daggerboard: Up; slides over the water with minimum foil friction.
  • Vang: Center; catches air volume without pinning the boom low.
  • Downhaul: Off; fully slack luff to gather light air flows.
  • Outhaul: Full; deep rounded envelope for maximum surface area.

Medium to Heavy Wind (8+ kn)

  • Boom Angle: 90° (lock square into deep wind vectors).
  • Sailor Position: Neutral (medium air) or Aft (heavy air) to balance wave friction.
  • Daggerboard: Up (medium wind) or Center (heavy wind) to maintain high-speed control.
  • Vang: Center; stabilizes the boom to stop sudden downwind death-rolls.
  • Downhaul: Off (medium wind) or Center (heavy wind); maintains draft entry profile.
  • Outhaul: Full; keeps a deep driving pocket to capture maximum wind energy.

4. Tactical Mastery: Reading Wind Shifts

Speed is useless if you are sailing the wrong way! When tracking upwind (Close Hauled), the wind will constantly shift. Your strategy must change instantly based on what the wind does.

💨 The Lift (Favorable Shift)

What it is: The wind shifts further behind your boat.

The Result: You can turn closer toward your target upwind mark without your sails flapping.

Strategy: Stay on this tack! Ride the lift as long as it lasts to steal a shorter path to the buoy.

💨 The Header (Unfavorable Shift)

What it is: The wind shifts straight into your bow.

The Result: You are forced to turn your boat away from your target upwind mark to keep your sails full.

Strategy: Tack immediately! A header on your current side means a lift is waiting for you on the other side.

🏆 The Golden Upwind Rule

"Tack on a header, stay on a lift!"

Sailing a straight line away from a shift leaves you stranded. Watch your compass angle, feel the header, and tack quickly to stay on the inside track.

5. Boat Handling: Maneuver Penalties

Sailing fast requires smooth boat handling. Avoid unnecessary turns and sloppy maneuvers, as jerky movements drag through the water and kill your forward momentum.
